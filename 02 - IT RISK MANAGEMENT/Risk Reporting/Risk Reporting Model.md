# Risk Reporting Model

## Purpose

Risk reporting communicates exposure, trend, treatment, and decisions. It should be short enough for management to use and supported by enough evidence for security and risk teams to defend.

## Reporting Structure

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Current State | What risk exists now |
| Target State | What acceptable state looks like |
| Gap | What prevents the target state |
| Business Impact | Why it matters |
| Treatment Options | Accept, mitigate, transfer, avoid |
| Recommendation | Professional judgment based on evidence |
| Residual Risk | What remains after action |
| Decision Required | Specific management choice |

## Reporting Levels

| Level | Audience | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Operational | Control owners, IT, security teams | Tickets, evidence, testing, remediation status |
| Tactical | Risk owners, security leadership | Risk trends, overdue items, treatment blockers |
| Executive | Executives / board | Material risks, decisions, investment, residual exposure |

## Unsupported Claim Check

Avoid these claims unless evidence exists:

| Claim | Evidence Needed |
|---|---|
| "Risk is low" | Approved methodology, control evidence, impact/likelihood rationale |
| "Control is effective" | Test evidence and scope |
| "Issue is remediated" | Retest or validation evidence |
| "Risk is accepted" | Approval, authority, date, expiry |
| "Compliant" | Formal scope and assessment evidence |
