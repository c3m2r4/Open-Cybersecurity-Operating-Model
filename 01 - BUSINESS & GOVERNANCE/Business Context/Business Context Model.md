# Business Context Model

## Executive Summary

Business context explains why a security topic matters. Without it, technical controls can become disconnected from the services, obligations, and outcomes they are meant to protect.

## What To Capture

| Area | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Business objective | What the organization is trying to achieve | VERIFY |
| Business process | The process that supports the objective | VERIFY |
| Critical assets | Systems, identities, data, facilities, third parties, or services required by the process | VERIFY |
| Stakeholders | Business owner, process owner, risk owner, control owner, technical owner | VERIFY |
| Impact categories | Financial, operational, legal, regulatory, safety, reputational, customer trust | VERIFY |
| Tolerance | How much disruption, loss, or exposure management can tolerate | NOT YET ESTABLISHED |

## Beginner Explanation

A business process is the work the organization needs to perform. Cybersecurity protects the technology and information that make that work possible. For example, MFA is not important because it is a fashionable security control; it is important because unauthorized account access can disrupt operations, expose data, and create fraud risk.

## Practitioner Use

Before writing a risk, control, finding, or executive summary, identify the business process and assets involved. If the business owner is unknown, mark it `VERIFY` rather than inventing ownership.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts |
|---|---|
| Starting with a tool instead of a process | The note may be technically interesting but irrelevant to decision-makers |
| Calling every system critical | Prioritization becomes meaningless |
| Ignoring data and identity assets | Many business impacts come through account misuse or data exposure |
| Assuming ownership | Risk acceptance requires an accountable owner |
