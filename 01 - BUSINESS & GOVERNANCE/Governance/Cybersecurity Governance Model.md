# Cybersecurity Governance Model

## Overview

Cybersecurity governance defines who makes security decisions, who owns risk, who operates controls, and how evidence is reviewed.

## Governance Roles

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Board / Executive Leadership | Set risk appetite, approve major investment, oversee material cyber risk |
| Business Owner | Own business impact and approve operational tradeoffs |
| Risk Owner | Decide treatment for assigned risks |
| Control Owner | Operate and maintain controls |
| Security Function | Advise, assess, monitor, validate, and report security risk |
| IT / Engineering Owner | Implement and maintain technical systems and remediation |
| Internal Audit / Assurance | Independently assess governance, risk, and control effectiveness where applicable |

## Decision Rights

| Decision | Typical Authority | Evidence Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Accept residual risk | Risk owner or delegated executive authority | Risk assessment, compensating controls, expiry, approval |
| Fund remediation | Business or executive leadership | Finding, impact, options, cost, risk reduction |
| Approve policy | Executive leadership or governance forum | Policy draft, scope, exceptions process |
| Decommission risky service | Business and technology leadership | Impact analysis, migration plan, residual risk |
| Change security architecture | Architecture governance / security leadership | Threat model, control requirements, design review |

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Risk |
|---|---|
| No clear risk owner | Risks remain unresolved or implicitly accepted |
| Security owns all risk on paper | Business impact decisions are made without business accountability |
| Policies without enforcement | Governance appears mature but controls fail in practice |
| Metrics without decisions | Reporting becomes informational instead of actionable |
